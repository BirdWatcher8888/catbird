    About the language — plainly, before you buy

    This is grown-up stand-up. Expect adult language and adult themes — roughly what late-night network television lets through, and nothing you wouldn't overhear on a busy street. It is not explicit-record territory: no slurs-for-shock, nothing risqué for its own sake, nobody in the crowd gets attacked. An evening ticketed show is an adult room by nature; we say this so no one is surprised, not to scare anyone off. Bring your funny bone, leave the little ones home.

    Wheelchair seating — the short terms

    Three terms, in plain English. Scroll to the end and the agree button wakes up — we want these read, not skimmed.

    What you're booking

    A dedicated wheelchair space on the Tier 2 cross-aisle (row K), sized for a wheelchair or mobility device, with clear sightlines and an aisle-side approach. Each space can pair with one adjacent companion seat so nobody watches alone. Both price at the ordinary Tier 2 rate — access never costs extra here.

    The terms

    1. Wheelchair spaces exist for patrons who attend in a wheelchair or mobility device. Booking one is your statement that it's for such a patron in your party. Nobody will ever ask you for proof of disability — not at checkout, not at the door. That is your business, not ours.

    2. The space itself is the seat: it fits a wheelchair, and it is where the wheelchair parks for the show. A patron arriving without one simply joins the general-admission zones with everyone else, and the space goes back to those waiting for it.

    3. A ticket obtained by misrepresentation is void and non-refundable — the wheelchair space and its paired companion seat are both forfeit (just the single seat, if booked alone). We would honestly rather eat the loss of two seats than see these spaces taken from the people they exist for. There is nothing to chase and nothing to dispute: the ticket is simply void, on terms you scrolled through and agreed to right here.
